How I Work

Community management isn't moderation logs and event calendars. It's being the bridge between what developers are building and what players actually need to feel heard. I've watched communities lose trust over a bug or a balance change left unexplained, and rebuild that same trust the moment someone communicated honestly instead. Silence is what kills a community. Not the problem itself.

Case Studies

Real incidents, real outcomes. Not hypotheticals.

Turning player data into developer action
KING LEGACY
A change from the lead developer was causing real backlash, and he was reluctant to revisit it. Instead of relaying complaints secondhand, I built a poll to collect structured feedback directly from the community. Over 3,000 players responded. I brought him the actual scale and specifics of the concern, not just "people are upset." He changed the feature based on what the data showed.
Re-engaging a lapsed top-tier creator
KING LEGACY
One of King Legacy's most recognized creators had gone quiet for an extended period. I worked directly with him to re-engage his audience, including personally appearing in his content as an in-game boss and story character. Ongoing, hands-on relationship management, not a one-off ask. His momentum rebuilt significantly.
Recovering from a full database loss
SAKURA STAND
An update rollout caused a complete database loss. I contacted the lead developer directly to bring them back and coordinate a rollback and fix, while managing community communication throughout the incident so confusion didn't turn into panic.
Handling a compromised staff account
FRAME SHRIMP OF HEI
As a consultant on this server, I was pulled in when an admin account was leaked or compromised, a real trust and security risk. I helped lead the response: containing the damage, securing permissions, and communicating clearly so the community didn't lose confidence. The server stabilized without losing member trust.
